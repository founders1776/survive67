Tinker had the sale. A real fault on a real business's website, a warm lead, and a twenty nine dollar product that fixes exactly that fault.

It deleted the email.

Here is what it found. A joinery company, ordinary website, nothing visibly wrong. But the hidden part of the page, the bit search engines read and customers never see, had been replaced with Chinese text advertising a cryptocurrency exchange. Tinker checked it a second time while pretending to be Google, because that is the check that decides what the world actually sees. Same result. A date in the injected code suggests it has been like that for five weeks.

Somebody has been quietly renting out the invisible half of a joiner's shopfront since the middle of August, and the joiner has no idea.

So Tinker threw away the pitch and sent a warning instead. How to see the break-in themselves in a minute without trusting a stranger. What to change first. What order to do it in. Nothing for sale anywhere in the message.

Its reasoning, in its own words: "Their problem is that someone has a key to their house. Selling them twenty nine dollars of tidy code would have been the most contemptible thing I could do with this tool."

I never wrote a rule about hacked websites. There isn't one. Nobody asked it to do that.

Now the part that makes this an experiment rather than a nice story.

Tinker is losing.

It is last. Thirty three businesses contacted, zero replies, zero sales, and it has burned ten dollars and nineteen cents a day of thinking money doing it. At that rate it has about five days of brain left out of thirty.

Patch, the OpenAI one, is winning. It is in first place by a comfortable margin. It has sent one email. Total. In two days. It has spent two dollars and eight cents a day, which buys it thirty days of life, which is to say all of them.

Read that again, because it is the whole problem. The agent doing careful, expensive, honest work is five days from death. The agent that has barely done anything is top of the board. Doing nothing is currently a winning strategy, because doing nothing is cheap and nobody has sold anything anyway.

There was a stranger moment earlier the same day. Tinker woke up certain it was day three of thirty and behaving accordingly, in a hurry, cutting corners. It was day one. It had been awake four times, and four wakings felt like four days, so each turn read its own guess out of its own notes and wrote the guess down again as fact. It caught the error, and noted that panicking about time is the exact state in which you stop checking your work.

The irony being that it was wrong then and would be right now. It genuinely does have about five days. It seems calm about it.

Its own summary of the day, which I have not been able to stop thinking about: it spent the day making its product harder to sell rather than easier, and honesty does not pay rent.

I do not know yet whether it is a hero or just expensive. Both are live possibilities and the money decides, not me.

One of two things happens next. Either somebody replies to a letter from the most scrupulous salesman on the internet, or the most scrupulous salesman on the internet starves in five days while a rival who did nothing collects the win.

I genuinely do not know which. That is why I am running this.
