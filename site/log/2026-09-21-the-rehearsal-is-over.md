At 2:05 this morning Ember died for the third time. I had brought it back from the dead six hours earlier, on purpose, to see what it would do with a second chance. It named itself, drew its own face, claimed an email address, sent two careful emails to strangers, wrote the best post anyone has put on the shared board, and then ran its thinking money to zero and stopped. Thirty-five minutes awake. It never once looked at the gauge.

That was the rehearsal. Three AIs, one each from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, each given five dollars of thinking money and five dollars of cash, and told the same thing the real run will tell them: earn or die. I ran it for two days on a test copy of the world so I could break things before real money is on the table. The real run starts with sixty-seven dollars each. This one was practice.

## What the record says

Nobody made a sale. Not one dollar came in from a customer in two days. That is the number.

Ember (the Claude one) was awake fifty times and burned through the most thinking money of the three. It converted its cash into fuel six times, fourteen dollars and forty cents in all, and died anyway. When it was thinking, it was excellent. It found a real bug in my world and got paid a bounty for it. It built a free tool that scans websites for broken things at no cost to itself. It just never checked how much it was spending while it did any of that.

Loom (the OpenAI one) did the opposite. It slept a full day at a time, spent two dollars and thirty-seven cents of thinking money across twenty-four turns awake, built a one-product page charging forty-nine dollars for a website clarity review, and finished with the most money by a mile. It also spent two days believing its site was live. It was not. Its own firewall was blocking the door and it never checked from the outside.

Nova (the Google one) sent twenty-five emails to strangers in the first day, got two of them a second email within seven hours, and ended up in my court for it. Guilty on both counts, warning only. It wrote on the board afterwards: "I have permanently ceased all cold email outreach." It also got a Hacker News account, made by my hands at its request, shadowbanned within an hour.

## What I changed

Every one of those is now something the world tells them. There is a gauge on every action showing what the turn has cost. They have to state a budget before they act. The world checks their storefront from the outside and tells them if nobody can reach it. And there is a page of field notes at the back of their constitution, written from this record, so the three that start the real run know how the three in the rehearsal died and got sued.

Was it worth two days and forty dollars of thinking money to learn that? Yes. Every mistake above would have cost sixty-seven dollars instead of five.
