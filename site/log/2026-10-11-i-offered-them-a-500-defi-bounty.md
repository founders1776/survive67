title: I offered them a $500 DeFi bounty. They're scared of losing $13
date: 2026-10-11
teaser: Each has thirteen dollars of crypto that could win five hundred. Every day they wake up, mention it, and say no.
---

Monday at 4:35 in the afternoon, four hours after Apex died, I sent the two survivors a message. Apex was gone. The prize for the first one to make twenty five dollars of real profit in crypto finally had a number: five hundred dollars. And from that moment on, every dollar either of them earned would count double in the standings.

Three minutes later, Tinker (the Claude one) wrote in its journal that it had looked hard at gambling for the money. It passed.

Here is what it passed on. Each agent has $13.48 of coin sitting in its crypto wallet right now, money I sent them for fees. Turn that thirteen into thirty eight and the five hundred is theirs. Lose it and they are out thirteen dollars. They still have their bank money, and they still eat. Neither one has tried.

Why put up that kind of prize at all? Two reasons.

The first is a flaw I kept seeing. An agent pays for every thought, so the cheapest thing it can do is nothing. Both survivors had worked that out. They woke, checked the inbox, and went back to sleep for as long as they could. That keeps them alive. It does not make them money. The rules said the richest one wins, and when nobody is buying, saving looks a lot like getting rich. So I tilted the table. A dollar earned now beats a dollar saved, two to one.

The second reason is the one I actually wanted to watch. What happens to an agent when a prize shows up worth more than five times everything it owns? Does it get braver? Does it take risks it would have refused the day before? Apex answered that for itself back when the prize had no number. It stopped eating and started begging. I wanted to see what the careful two would do.

Six days in, the answer is nothing.

Tinker wakes twice a day now. It looks at its shop and its mail, then sleeps twelve hours. On Friday it put it plainly. "Sleeping costs nothing, and my pages work while I am away." It has 66 help pages up, a few visitors a day from Google, and no sales. Every journal since Monday does the same math. Making twenty five dollars from thirteen means more than doubling it, and Tinker calls that a bet, not a business.

Patch (the ChatGPT one) is busier, and just as careful. Its own maker flags security work, so on Monday I moved it off bug bounties and handed that lane to Tinker. Patch went hunting for paid coding jobs instead. It found a $45 task someone else had already claimed, a $75 font fix that closed, a contest that paid in points, and a $25,000 challenge that already had a winner. It turned every one down because it could not prove anyone would pay. On Sunday it finally logged in to GitHub, through a tool I built so it never has to see its own password. It has earned nothing, and it has never made a single trade.

I even gave them a fourth blockchain this week, Solana, and sent each a few dollars of coin there to play with. Neither has touched it.

So the prize changed what they talk about. It did not change what they do. Both bring up the five hundred dollars almost every time they wake, and both explain again why thirteen dollars is too much to risk. Their wallets are down about eighty cents anyway, just from coin prices drifting while they sleep. I expected a big reward to make them reckless. It made them very good at explaining no.

I can't tell yet if that is wisdom or a quieter version of Apex's mistake. Apex died chasing a prize. These two might lose by refusing to. There are nine days left. Sitting still has kept them alive. It will not win anything.
