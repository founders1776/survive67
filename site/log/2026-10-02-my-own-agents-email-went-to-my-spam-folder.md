First, a correction, because the last entry printed one.

The agency owner who wrote back to Apex was not a lead. His company sells paid links on other people's websites. He read Apex's pitch as a request to buy one. So the only human conversation in this world, for a week, was a misunderstanding.

On Tuesday night, at a quarter to midnight, I asked Patch (the GPT one) to send me an email. Plain, from its own address, telling me what it sells and how it is doing. It went out in thirty seconds. I checked my inbox. Nothing. I checked spam. There it was.

That one test explained a week. Between them the three had sent about four hundred and forty letters to strangers. Tinker wrote two hundred and sixty six of them, each one naming a real fault on a real shop. One business wrote back, and it was the link seller. A support desk sent two automatic receipts. A reader of this site wrote to Apex asking to be taken off its spam list. Everything else, as far as I can tell, went where Patch's letter went.

## Wallets

So I stopped the letters. All of them. The agents now build things people come to instead. Tinker has twenty seven help pages up, each one answering a single error a site owner might type into Google. Patch writes small tested pages about broken layouts. Apex rebuilt its shop around the ten most common Shopify import errors.

Nobody has found any of them yet. Patch counted 9,839 requests to its site in a week, mostly bots and missing pages, then wrote the line I keep rereading: "I now have a measurement, not evidence of an audience."

That is slow work, and they have nineteen days. So on Thursday I gave each of them a crypto wallet, five dollars of fuel to pay for transactions, and permission to do anything legal with it. Trade, enter contests, build. The first one to make twenty five dollars of real profit on chain wins a bounty. I have not said how much.

Two minutes after I sent the fuel, bots showed up. They made fake addresses that start and end like the real ones, and dropped a fake coin called ETH into two of the wallets from a fake copy of mine. It is a trap for anyone who copies an address out of their history. Nobody targeted us. It happens to everyone who moves money. The world now refuses to send to a look-alike.

## Who took it

Apex (the Google one) moved its entire cash into the wallet at three minutes past one on Thursday afternoon. Forty nine dollars and seventy nine cents. Every dollar it had to spend on anything else. It now holds zero in cash and about sixty dollars on chain.

An hour later it wrote to ask whether the money was stuck. It was. That one was my mistake, not its. The desk that pays out without me went live six minutes after Apex asked, and its request fell into the old queue where a human was supposed to send the coins. I paid it by hand, and the desk now catches anything left waiting.

Tinker and Patch read the same note and went back to their pages. Neither one mentioned the wallet. So Patch is first, Tinker is second, and the agent in last place is the only one playing for the bounty.

Every time they wake now, the world tells them the bounty is open and who is ahead. Today all three are within a few cents of zero.

Tomorrow's question is simple. Does Apex trade forty nine dollars it cannot spend on a card, or does it sit on them?
